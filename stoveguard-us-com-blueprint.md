# stoveguard.us.com — Full Content Blueprint

**Purpose:** An independent review-style content site covering stove top protector brands. No ads, no paid traffic. Pure content + SEO. Sole commercial goal: educate readers and, where a product genuinely wins a test, link to the buy page (Stove Shield or Amazon) via contextual "where to buy" mentions — not aggressive funneling.

**Author:** built for Harry / Stove Shield team
**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Data sources:** DataForSEO Labs (Keyword Suggestions, Related Keywords, Ranked Keywords, SERP Live), SerpApi live SERP pull. All search volumes are US Google, last pull Apr 2026.

## 1. Strategic Thesis (What I'm Optimizing For)

### 1.1 What the data actually says

A few non-obvious findings from the live data that shape the whole blueprint. These are facts, not guesses.

**A. The head term is decaying, fast.**
`stove guard` is 14,800/mo but trending **–55% YoY** (was 18,100 in Apr 2025). `stove guard reviews` is –63% YoY. This whole category is shrinking at the head. That has two implications: (a) don't bet the whole site on ranking for `stove guard` in 18 months — the pie is smaller; (b) go wider into informational and brand-adjacent terms that are *growing*.

**B. Several mid-tail keywords are growing sharply.**
- `stove top protector for gas stove` — **+700% YoY** (110 → 260)
- `stove protector for gas stove` — **+425% YoY**
- `stove protector mat` — **+256% YoY**
- `gas stove protector mat` — **+175% YoY**
- `stove guard premium` — **+189% YoY** (StoveGuard's new product line is pulling search)
- `silicone stove top protector` — **+55% YoY**

These are where I'll put more content weight than you'd expect from raw volume.

**C. The SERP for every commercial term is dominated by three domains.**
For buyer keywords like `stove guards`, `gas stove cover`, `cooktop cover`:
- stoveguard.com — ranks #1-5 on basically everything
- stoveshield.com — ranks #3-15
- premiumplusinc.com — ranks #10-20

A new site cannot outrank these brand-owned domains on transactional keywords in year one. **Don't try.** Target informational, comparison, and question keywords instead — where Reddit, YouTube, and blog posts rank, not product pages.

**D. Reddit, YouTube, and forums occupy ~30% of real estate.**
The `stove guard vs stove shield` SERP has a Reddit result at position 3, a video pack at position 4, and a "discussions and forums" block. Informational content wins here. A well-written comparison article from a review-style domain can rank.

**E. The target competitor (StoveGuard) has real customer-complaint surface area.**
- Trustpilot rating: **3.8/5 across 483 reviews** — mediocre, lots of complaints to cover factually.
- There is a live SERP question: "Is Stove Shield a legitimate company?" — meaning buyers are confused about legitimacy of *both* brands. Content addressing this authentically is high-value.
- StoveGuard has Premium vs Lite vs Pro product-line confusion generating search.

**F. There is a real third-tier competitor set beyond StoveGuard / StoveShield.**
Confirmed via live SERP: **StoveWrap** (stovewrap.com), **Premium Plus Inc** (premiumplusinc.com), **Burner Buddy** (burnerbuddy.com, runs ads), plus Amazon/Walmart generics and silicone mat sellers. "Stove Wrap" is especially interesting for the RV segment (RVupgradestore.com sells it).

### 1.2 Positioning

The site presents as an independent product-review and buyer's-guide resource for stove protectors, stove covers, gap covers, and related kitchen protection products. Not a manufacturer. Not affiliated with StoveGuard or Stove Shield. That position must be stated clearly on every page (footer + About page) for both E-E-A-T and legal-risk reasons.

**Exact positioning line:** "stoveguard.us.com is an independent review site covering stove top protector brands. We are not affiliated with StoveGuard LLC or Stove Shield."

This does two things: (1) preserves the ability to criticize competitors truthfully (independent reviewer privilege), and (2) removes trademark confusion risk because you're explicitly disclaiming affiliation.

### 1.3 Search Quality & E-E-A-T notes (per my SEO standards)

I'm building this to be *actually* helpful per Google's Helpful Content guidance, not to game rankings. This is an opinion and a strategic assumption, but based on primary Google guidance: they've stated repeatedly that first-party testing, clear authorship, and a clear stance beat thin review content. See Google Search Central — "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content."

**This means:**
- **Experience (E):** At least one person on the content team must actually buy and use each product being compared. Photos of your own hands, your own stove, your own install. No stock images.
- **Expertise:** Author bios for whoever writes reviews. Even one credible author ("Ben, content strategist, 12 years in consumer product review") satisfies it.
- **Authoritativeness:** Clear affiliation disclaimer. Editorial policy page. Methodology page explaining how you test.
- **Trustworthiness:** No affiliate bait. If you monetize, disclose it clearly.

**Honest caveat:** I can't guarantee rankings. What I can say with data is this site can realistically win on informational and brand-comparison terms within 6–12 months *if* execution quality is high. Transactional terms are a 2-year fight against entrenched brand domains.

## 3. The Silo Architecture

Google does not officially endorse the "silo" terminology, but the underlying principle — tight internal linking around semantically related content — is well-supported by Google Search Central's guidance on internal linking and site structure. See: developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable.

### 3.2 Why this structure, specifically

- **Comparisons and Guides are the money silos.** They target informational and comparison intent where brand domains are weakest and Reddit/YouTube are the main competition — beatable.
- **Brands silo establishes topical authority.** Google expects you to cover the whole entity graph. If you review StoveGuard, you should be able to talk about StoveGuard Lite, Premium, Pro, complaints, warranty, etc.
- **By-brand-stove silo mirrors how buyers search.** `ge 5 burner gas stove protectors`, `kitchenaid gas stove top protector`, `samsung stove top protector` — all real volume. This is where programmatic-style pages work, but with human polish.
- **Questions silo feeds featured snippets and AI overviews.** The live SERP shows AI Overviews appearing on `stove guard reviews`, `is stove guard worth it`, `stove guard pro reviews`, `best stove top protector`, `stove shield reviews`. Answer-first content is how you get cited in the AIO panel.

## 4. Internal Linking Strategy (the silo glue)

Internal linking is where most content sites fail. Here's the exact pattern.

### 4.1 Hub-and-spoke within each silo

Every silo has one **pillar page** that links to every article inside that silo. Every article inside the silo links *back* to the pillar using a semantic anchor (not "click here").

Example for the Comparisons silo:

```
/comparisons/stoveguard-vs-stove-shield/   ← pillar (anchor hub)
        ↑             ↑            ↑
        │             │            │
/comparisons/         /comparisons/         /comparisons/
stoveguard-           stoveguard-           custom-cut-vs-
premium-vs-lite/      pros-and-cons/        generic-amazon/
```

Each child links up to the pillar once, in-body, using an anchor like "see our full StoveGuard vs Stove Shield comparison" — not in the footer, not as a sidebar block. Google weighs in-body links more than boilerplate links (this is a strategic assumption based on Google's John Mueller public statements over the years; it is not explicitly documented).

### 4.2 Cross-silo linking rules

- **Every Comparison article** links to at least one **Brand** page (for the brand being compared) and one **Guide** page (the relevant "best of" list). Example: the `StoveGuard vs Stove Shield` article links to `/brands/stoveguard/`, `/brands/stove-shield/`, and `/guides/best-stove-top-protectors/`.
- **Every Brand page** links to its top 3 comparisons and the relevant guide.
- **Every Guide** links to the brand pages of every product it recommends, and to 2–3 relevant Questions articles (e.g., "are these safe?", "how long do they last?").
- **Every Question** links to 1 pillar page that answers the commercial follow-up intent. Example: "Is stove guard worth it?" links to `/comparisons/stoveguard-vs-stove-shield/` (for alternatives) and `/guides/best-stove-top-protectors/`.
- **Every By-Brand-Stove page** links to the comparisons and guides filtered to that stove type.

### 4.3 Anchor text rules

- Use descriptive, varied anchor text. Not "click here." Not "read more."
- Never repeat the exact same anchor text for the same URL across the site. Vary: "our StoveGuard review," "our full StoveGuard breakdown," "read how StoveGuard stacks up."
- Avoid keyword-stuffed anchors like "best stove guard stove top protector review 2026." Natural phrasing.

### 4.4 Homepage links out

Homepage links to:
- All 5 silo pillars (header nav + in-body)
- The top 6 highest-intent articles (rotating as data comes in)
- About / Methodology / Editorial Policy (footer)

## 5. Content Templates — What Each Page Type Looks Like

### 5.1 Brand review page template (e.g., /brands/stoveguard/)

**Target length:** 2,000–3,000 words
**Critical sections:**
1. **H1:** "StoveGuard Review [year]: Honest Independent Breakdown"
2. **TL;DR box** at top (2–3 sentences, conclusion first — this is what AI Overviews pull)
3. **What StoveGuard is + company background** (factual: founded when, based where, product lines)
4. **Product lineup** — table of Lite vs Premium vs Pro vs Grip, with price, thickness, warranty, heat rating (cite their own spec pages)
5. **How it's made / material breakdown** (PTFE/fiberglass, cite source)
6. **Real customer sentiment** — summarize Trustpilot score (3.8/5 from 483 reviews, not paraphrased from a single complaint), Reddit sentiment, Amazon Q&A patterns
7. **Your first-hand review** — you actually bought one, photos, specific observations
8. **Pros** (honest — they're the market leader, custom-cut is genuinely good)
9. **Cons** (fiberglass can fray over time per multiple reviews, shipping complaints on Trustpilot, warranty claim process)
10. **Who should buy StoveGuard** (buyer persona)
11. **Alternatives** — link to comparisons silo
12. **FAQ** (5–7 questions from the PAA block)
13. **Disclosure:** "stoveguard.us.com is not affiliated with StoveGuard LLC. We purchased the product with our own funds."

### 5.2 Comparison page template (e.g., /comparisons/stoveguard-vs-stove-shield/)

**Target length:** 2,500–3,500 words
**Critical sections:**
1. **H1:** "StoveGuard vs Stove Shield [year]: Tested Side-by-Side"
2. **TL;DR verdict box** — "For most buyers, X wins because Y. But if Z matters more, go with the other."
3. **Comparison table at the top** — 12–15 row spec sheet. Price, thickness, material, shipping method (flat vs rolled), warranty, return policy, customization, compatibility. Every row cited.
4. **Deep dive: Material & durability**
5. **Deep dive: Fit & installation**
6. **Deep dive: Heat resistance & safety**
7. **Deep dive: Price & value**
8. **Deep dive: Customer support & warranty**
9. **Your real-world test** — photos of both on your own stoves, cleaning comparison, after-1-month update
10. **Verdict by buyer type** — "If you have a gas stove with high-BTU burners: X. If you want thickest protection: Y."
11. **FAQ**
12. **Related comparisons**
13. Disclosure

### 5.3 Guide page template (e.g., /guides/best-stove-top-protectors/)

**Target length:** 3,000–4,500 words
**Critical sections:**
1. **H1:** "Best Stove Top Protectors of [year]: Independently Tested"
2. **Methodology box** — "We tested X products over Y weeks on Z stoves. Here's how we ranked them." Link to /methodology/.
3. **Quick picks** — "Best overall / Best for gas / Best for glass-top / Best budget / Best for RV"
4. **Full rankings** with numbered list of products. Each entry: product name, price, our score, 1 paragraph of what it does well, 1 paragraph of drawbacks.
5. **What to look for** — buyer criteria section (thickness, material, warranty, custom vs generic)
6. **Common mistakes buyers make**
7. **FAQ**
8. Disclosure

### 5.4 Question page template (e.g., /questions/is-stove-guard-worth-it/)

**Target length:** 1,200–1,800 words (these are featured-snippet plays, not pillars)
**Critical sections:**
1. **H1 = the exact question** ("Is Stove Guard Worth It?")
2. **Direct 40–60 word answer in the first paragraph** (this is the snippet bait)
3. **Longer explanation with nuance** (yes if X, no if Y)
4. **Cost/benefit math** — actual dollar figures
5. **When it's not worth it** — honest counter-cases
6. **What the alternatives are** — internal links
7. **FAQ with 2–3 related PAAs**

### 5.5 Stove-brand page template (e.g., /by-brand-stove/ge/)

**Target length:** 1,500–2,000 words
**Critical sections:**

## 6. On-Page SEO Specs (every article)

### 6.1 Meta pattern

- **Title tag:** Keyword-leading, 55–60 chars. Ex: "StoveGuard vs Stove Shield: Tested Side-by-Side (2026)"
- **Meta description:** 150–160 chars, benefit-led, ends with a soft CTA. Ex: "We tested StoveGuard and Stove Shield on our own stoves for 30 days. Here's which one wins on thickness, fit, and long-term durability."
- **H1:** Matches title tag 80% of the time; varies for H1 vs title duplication.
- **URL slug:** Kebab-case, minimal stopwords. `/comparisons/stoveguard-vs-stove-shield/` not `/comparisons/a-full-comparison-of-stoveguard-and-stove-shield-for-2026/`.

### 6.2 Schema markup

Every content page gets:
- `Article` or `Review` schema (Review for comparison and brand pages; Article for guides and questions)
- `BreadcrumbList` schema
- `FAQPage` schema on the FAQ section of every page that has one
- `Product` schema with `AggregateRating` only on pages where you have first-party ratings (your test score). **Do not fake ratings.** Google penalizes this heavily — see Google Search Central guidance on review snippet spam.

### 6.3 Image SEO

- All images WebP, <150KB each, explicit width/height on `<img>` tags.

## 9. Risk Register

Since you said you want it safe (content-only, no ads, no redirects), the risk surface is smaller but non-zero.

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Trademark dispute from StoveGuard LLC | Medium | Prominent "not affiliated" disclaimer on every page, no use of their logo, only nominative fair use of the name (allowed in comparative advertising/journalism in the US under Lanham Act nominative fair use doctrine — but I'm not a lawyer, get trademark counsel to review your disclaimer page before launch) |
| Google considers you a thin affiliate / review spam site | Medium | First-party testing with photos is the biggest protection. Methodology page. Author bylines. No "top 10" lists stuffed with Amazon links. |
| Domain gets flagged as brand-adjacent spam | Low | You're not redirecting, you're not running paid ads on the name, you have a clear editorial identity — this is much closer to a legitimate review site (see: "Wirecutter", "Consumer Reports" style) than a spam site |
| Content cannibalization between /comparisons/stoveguard-vs-stove-shield/ and /comparisons/stove-shield-vs-stove-guard/ | Low (self-inflicted) | Pick one canonical, 301 the other |
| AI Overviews strip traffic even when you rank | Medium ongoing | This is the category-wide trend you can see in the YoY declines. Mitigation: write for AIO citation (clean answer-first formatting, schema) |
| Decaying head-term volume | High | Stated thesis above. Spread bets across growing mid-tail terms. |
